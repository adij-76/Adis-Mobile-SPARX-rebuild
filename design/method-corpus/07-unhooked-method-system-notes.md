## 1. Practitioner_Manual_v0.2.docx

Provenance: Drive fileId `1naB-tagrMCvMzkNM4r-UBPF44r98nvmP`, folder
`Unhooked-Method-System/manual/`, docx, 27,537 bytes, modified 2026-04-24,
read in full 2026-07-07.

Full title: **"The Unhooked Method — A Practitioner Manual — Hooks, SPARO, and
EAT for Substance Use Disorder"** (Adi Jaffe, PhD, IGNTD, Version 0.2, April
2026). First formal manualized version of the method; maps a 12-session
clinical protocol onto the book's structure. Sessions 1 and 5 are full worked
examples; Sessions 2-4 and 6-12 are template-only pending Phase 1 drafting.

### Front matter (summary)

- Audience: licensed clinicians, certified addiction counselors, supervised
  peer recovery specialists; a separate Coach-level version planned.
- Required training: "Completion of the Unhooked Method Clinician training
  (minimum 20 CE hours) plus 10 supervised cases before independent practice."
- Scope: "A 12-session outpatient protocol for adults with substance use
  disorders, deliverable individually, in group (see Appendix C), or via
  structured telehealth." Complements (not replaces) SUD medications incl.
  naltrexone, acamprosate, buprenorphine, methadone, GLP-1 receptor agonists.
- What it is not (verbatim list): "Not abstinence-mandated — harm-reduction
  goal structures are supported and manualized. / Not a substitute for
  medically supervised withdrawal. / Not appropriate as sole treatment in
  active psychosis, acute mania, or imminent suicide risk. / Not a
  trauma-processing protocol — trauma-informed but not trauma-focused."

### Core premise (Ch. 1, verbatim)

> "This manual is built on a different starting assumption: substance use is a
> highly adaptive response to a specific set of reinforcing emotional,
> relational, cognitive, and environmental conditions unique to that
> individual. Addiction is a syndrome — a convergent presentation with
> divergent origins — not a unitary disease. Durable change requires
> identifying and addressing those origins. The behavior is the solution the
> person found for an underlying problem. Treat the underlying problem (the
> hook), and the behavior loses its function."

### Hooks (Ch. 2) — definitions verbatim

Core definition:

> "A hook is the underlying emotional, psychological, or situational driver
> that makes a behavior persist. The behavior is not the hook. The behavior is
> what the hook uses. Every persistent compulsive or self-destructive behavior
> exists because it is solving a problem — often one the person is no longer
> consciously aware of."

The diagnostic question:

> "Not 'how do I stop doing this?' but 'what is this doing for me?'"

The origin principle:

> "Hooks are almost always formed at the beginning of the behavior, not at the
> end. ... The start is often lost in the outcome — obscured by years of
> behavioral layering. A core clinical skill in this method is recovering the
> origin."

Clinical implications (verbatim headings): "Behavior-first treatment produces
substitution." / "Shame reinforces the loop. Shame activates the same
defensive circuits that drive the hook. Accept is mechanistically required,
not motivationally nice." / "Understanding precedes intervention."

### SPARO — the behavioral chain (Ch. 3)

> "SPARO is the five-stage chain underlying hook-driven behavior. Every
> persistent pattern follows this sequence, and every stage is a potential
> intervention point."

The five stages, definitions verbatim:

- **S — Stimulus**: "The trigger: an external event, internal state, memory,
  or sensation. The match that lights the fuse." Clinical task: "build
  stimulus awareness and, where possible, stimulus modification — reduce
  exposure to avoidable triggers, build tolerance for unavoidable ones."
- **P — Perception**: "How the stimulus is interpreted, shaped by history,
  beliefs, identity, and cognitive biases. ... Perception is where childhood
  scripts, belief systems, and cognitive distortions do their work."
  Assessed "via the 5 Belief Buckets and 9 cognitive biases."
- **A — Activation**: "The internal emotional and physiological response that
  follows perception. The body's alarm system. Clinically rated on the
  Activation Thermometer (0-100): the Initial Activation Level (IAL) is the
  baseline; the safe zone is the range in which deliberate choice is
  available; the flooding threshold is the point above which rational thinking
  is overwhelmed and default survival responses take over. Most 'bad
  decisions' happen in a flooded state — the person isn't choosing poorly,
  they have temporarily lost access to choice."
- **R — Response**: "The behavior that relieves the activation. This is the
  'bad habit' — the drinking, the avoidance, the micromanaging, the emotional
  eating. It is the solution the person found for the activation, not the
  problem." Clinical task: "expand the menu of available responses, then
  select replacements using the P/I Ratio."
- **O — Outcome**: "The result of the response, which feeds back into the
  chain as a new stimulus. Outcomes either reinforce the hook (creating a
  loop) or, with intentional work, begin to break it."

Where the hook lives (verbatim):

> "The hook is located at the Perception–Activation junction. A Stimulus
> triggers a Perception filter; the filter fires Activation; the Activation
> drives the Response. Changing the perception of the stimulus, or resolving
> what the activation is pointing to, produces behavioral change downstream
> without requiring the client to 'force' the change at the Response stage."

The four intervention points (verbatim):

> "Change is possible at any stage. Stimulus work (trigger awareness and
> environmental modification). Perception work (reframing interpretation via
> the 9 cognitive biases and 5 belief buckets). Activation work (tolerance,
> regulation, affect labeling, the Spheres of Influence exercise). Response
> work (replacement behavior design via the P/I Ratio and implementation
> intentions). The most durable change addresses more than one stage
> simultaneously."

"Start at the Outcome" clinical approach (verbatim):

> "This method deliberately begins SPARO work at the Outcome — where the
> client arrives in pain — then walks backward through Response, Activation,
> Perception, and Stimulus to reveal the hook. This is counterintuitive: most
> approaches begin at Response (stop the behavior) or Stimulus (avoid
> triggers). Starting at Outcome honors where the client actually is, builds
> their own understanding organically, and produces therapeutic alliance
> earlier and more reliably than confrontational or goal-first entry points.
> It is a signature procedural feature of the Unhooked Method."

### EAT — the transformation cycle (Ch. 4)

> "EAT is the transformation process applied at each stage of the SPARO chain
> where a hook is discovered. It is not a one-time exercise — it is applied
> iteratively: Explore the stage, Accept what is found, Transform the pattern."

- **Explore** (verbatim): "Trace the behavior back to its origin; find when
  and why it started; understand what it was solving. Exploration requires
  curiosity without judgment. The clinician's stance: 'When did this start?
  What was happening in your life? What need was this meeting?' The client's
  task: witness their own pattern without yet trying to change it."
- **Accept** (verbatim): "Reduce the shame, self-criticism, and defensive
  contraction around the hook. This is not a soft prerequisite — it is a
  structural requirement. Self-criticism activates the threat system
  (amygdala-driven), which narrows thinking, increases reactivity, and blocks
  learning. You cannot change what you are defending against. Accept creates
  the neurological conditions — specifically, activation of the soothing
  system — that make Transform possible."

**Accept's three elements and tools (verbatim tool list):**

1. *Shame Reduction* — "Tool: Shame Resilience (Brown, 2010; 2012) — empathy
   as the antidote to shame." / "Tool: Externalization (White & Epston,
   Narrative Therapy) — 'I am an addict' → 'I have been struggling with
   addiction.'"
2. *Forgiveness (self and others)* — "Tool: Unsent Expression Letter
   (Pennebaker, expressive writing). Precursor exercise — the full weight of
   the wound must be acknowledged before forgiveness is possible." / "Tool:
   Ho'oponopono — the four phrases (I'm sorry. Please forgive me. Thank you.
   I love you.) directed at the self, the memory, or the situation. Intervenes
   at the Perception–Activation junction." / "Tool: REACH Model (Worthington).
   A 5-step empirically validated forgiveness process." / "Tool: The Interview
   Technique — structured self-administered interview from a third-party
   stance, with imaginative empathy filling in the gaps of what the client
   doesn't know." / "Tool: Role Reversal Writing — writing in first person as
   the person who hurt the client (Moreno psychodrama lineage; Emotion-Focused
   Therapy)." / "Tool: The Funeral Preparation Exercise — two-part mortality
   salience practice (own funeral, their funeral) grounded in Terror
   Management Theory and ACT values work." / "Conceptual frame: The Grief Work
   Reframe — unforgiveness as incomplete grief rather than justified anger.
   Reframes 'should I forgive?' (moral, defensive) as 'am I ready to stop
   carrying this grief?' (emotional, open)."
3. *Compassion* — "Tool: Self-Compassion Practice (Neff, 2003, 2021). The
   three components — Mindfulness, Common Humanity, Self-Kindness. A 3-5
   minute guided practice script is in the Tools appendix."

Integration note (verbatim): "the three Accept elements are mutually
reinforcing. Shame reduction creates safety to attempt forgiveness;
forgiveness dissolves the activation shame was feeding on; compassion provides
the ongoing internal climate that makes both possible. Self-compassion is the
strongest entry point for most clients because it is brief, somatic, and does
not require readiness to forgive."

- **Transform** (verbatim): "With the hook named (Explore) and shame dissolved
  (Accept), Transform is where the actual behavioral substitution happens. It
  has four elements, sequenced in the moment of activation (elements 1-3) and
  built between moments (element 4)."

**Transform's four elements (verbatim capture):**

1. *Naming the Activation* — "Name the emotion specifically, in the moment.
   Lieberman et al. (2007) demonstrated via fMRI that explicit affect labeling
   reduces amygdala activity and engages the prefrontal cortex. Specificity
   matters — 'stressed' is too broad; 'urgency because I don't have the
   clarity I need' is specific enough for the PFC to do the regulatory work.
   Labeling is an internal practice — the neurological benefit does not
   require external disclosure." Tools: "Affect Labeling (Lieberman et al.,
   2007)"; "The Labeling Meditation (Jaffe) — 5-10 minutes of habitual
   labeling practice so labeling becomes automatic under high-stakes stimuli."
2. *Reappraisal* — "Tool: Cognitive Reappraisal (Gross, 1998; Gross & John,
   2003) — change the interpretation of the situation, not the expression of
   the emotion." / "Tool: Arousal Reappraisal / Stress-as-Enhancing Mindset
   (Jamieson, Nock & Mendes, 2012; Crum, Salovey & Achor, 2013) — reinterpret
   the physiological arousal as preparation rather than threat. Shifts the
   cardiovascular response from a threat pattern to a challenge pattern."
3. *Replacement Response Design* — "You cannot remove a behavior — you can
   only substitute it. The hook that the original behavior was serving still
   exists. Replacement Response Design asks: what is this behavior doing for
   me, and what else could serve that function?" Tools: "The Replacement
   Concept (Unhooked Ch. 9). Four stages: identify the function, generate
   candidate replacements (5 maximum, to avoid decision paralysis), test for
   fit via behavioral experimentation, build the new chain deliberately." /
   "Implementation Intentions (Gollwitzer, 1999; Gollwitzer & Sheeran, 2006).
   Formula: 'When [Stimulus], I will [new Response].' Stated before the
   triggering situation. Works best when triggered by an early-warning
   activation signal, before flooding threshold." / "P/I Ratio Applied to the
   New Behavior. Across the 4 domains — Psychology (belief/narrative shifts),
   Biology (sleep, movement, nutrition as baseline activation levers),
   Environment (choice architecture), Spirituality (values alignment)." /
   "Behavioral Activation & Tracking. Rate activation before and after each
   deployment of the replacement behavior. Pattern emerges within 2-3 weeks:
   the replacement is working, or it is not, and the data makes it visible."
4. *Modeling* — "Modeling operates between activation moments, building the
   library of replacement responses. Active modeling — identifying a specific
   practice in someone you respect, adopting it as a time-limited experiment,
   and assessing whether it serves the hook's underlying function — is
   behavioral prototyping." Grounding: Bandura's Social Learning Theory
   (1977, 1986); Ibarra (1999) "provisional selves." Tool: "Leadership
   Modeling Framework (Jaffe). Five steps: identify the model (near-peer
   preferred over aspirational), extract the specific observable behavior, run
   a time-limited experiment, assess fit against the hook function, adapt and
   integrate."

### How SPARO and EAT interlock (Ch. 5, verbatim)

> "SPARO maps the what — the behavioral chain. EAT provides the how — the
> transformation process at each link. In combined practice: (1) identify the
> SPARO chain for a specific behavior; (2) apply EAT at the stage where
> intervention is most accessible or impactful; (3) the Outcome feeds back
> into the system — new outcomes become new stimuli, and the cycle either
> reinforces the old pattern or builds a new one; (4) over time, with
> deliberate practice, the new pattern becomes self-sustaining. This is the
> flywheel effect."

P/I Ratio (verbatim):

> "The P/I Ratio is the practical decision-making tool at the Response stage.
> For any behavior, there are Promoters (factors making it more likely) and
> Inhibitors (factors making it less likely), distributed across 4 domains:
> Psychology, Biology, Environment, Spirituality. Change happens by reducing
> Promoters of the target behavior and increasing Promoters of the replacement
> behavior. The ratio is dynamic — it changes with circumstances, stress, and
> life events — and must be reassessed continuously."

### Treatment structure (Part 2, summary + key verbatim)

- Intake: structured 75-minute intake in the week before Session 1. Battery:
  DSM-5 SUD module; Timeline Follow-Back (past 90 days); modality-appropriate
  craving scale (PACS alcohol / QSU nicotine / DDQ opioids-stimulants); Hooks
  Inventory v1 (in development); Activation Thermometer baseline (current IAL,
  typical daily range, flooding-episode frequency past 30 days); PHQ-9, GAD-7,
  PCL-5; WHOQOL-BREF; Success Index baseline; medication inventory. Intake
  ends with the Treatment Frame one-pager and informed consent.
- Session format (verbatim sequence): "Twelve weekly 50-minute sessions ...
  Opening check-in (5 min): mood, past-week use, medication adherence, safety.
  / Session review and homework review (5 min). / Focused phase-specific work
  (30 min). / Integration and homework setting (7 min). / Closure (3 min):
  scheduling, logistics, safety planning if indicated."

### Session 1 worked example (protocol, verbatim where marked)

Objective: establish frame, alliance, introduce Hooks + "Start at the
Outcome," form a preliminary hook hypothesis, secure goal-structure agreement.

Session plan (verbatim sequence): "Opening and alliance (10 min). ... Frame
setting (10 min). The three teaching moves, in this order: (1) 'Your behavior
is not the problem — it's your current solution. The work is to find out what
problem it's solving.' (2) 'We're going to work this backward. You're here
because of the consequences — the outcomes. We're going to start at the
outcomes and walk backward to discover the hook.' (3) 'This isn't about
willpower. It's about understanding a chain you've been running without
knowing it.' / Hook hypothesis (20 min). Review the client's Hooks Inventory
results and their narrative history. Name 2-3 candidate hooks collaboratively
— not as diagnoses, as hypotheses. Frame: 'Here's what I'm seeing. Tell me
where I'm right and where I'm wrong.' / Goal alignment (7 min). Confirm the
client's chosen treatment goal (abstinence, moderation, harm reduction), time
horizon, and the specific indicators of progress they will track. This is not
a commitment to change the behavior — it is a commitment to do the
investigation. / Homework (3 min). Three instances in the coming week where
one of the candidate hooks shows up. Observer stance only — no attempt to
change the behavior yet. Bring to Session 2."

Key teaching moves (manual instructs "use near-verbatim"):

> "The substance isn't the problem. It's the current solution to a problem we
> haven't named yet."
> "You have two or three hooks that have been running the show. Once we make
> them visible, we can work on them directly."
> "This treatment doesn't require you to promise anything about use yet. It
> requires you to observe — honestly — what shows up."
> "We start at the outcomes because that's where you actually are. Most
> treatments start by telling you to stop. We start by understanding."

Fidelity criteria (summary): clinician named Hooks/SPARO/"Start at the
Outcome"; client left with 2-3 candidate hooks and an explicit realistic goal;
no moralizing/pathologizing/unsolicited abstinence frame; EAT stance
("curiosity without judgment") held throughout. Pitfalls: enumerating all
hooks; imposing abstinence; over-teaching; moving too fast to Response talk.

### Session 5 worked example (Stimulus work, verbatim where marked)

Objective: map the stimulus landscape, identify highest-predictive external
and internal triggers, introduce stimulus modification ("remove the
removable"), preview Spheres of Influence, begin EAT at the Stimulus stage.

Session plan (verbatim sequence): "Opening check-in and homework review (10
min). Pay specific attention to any stimuli that appeared in multiple homework
entries — those are high-value intervention targets. / Stimulus mapping (15
min). Map the client's top 5 triggers by frequency and by activation
magnitude. External stimuli: people, locations, times, specific cues. Internal
stimuli: memories, physical sensations, emotional states. / EAT applied to
Stimulus (15 min). Explore: what does this stimulus remind the client of? What
does it mean to them (Perception preview)? Accept: the stimulus exists.
Shaming oneself for being triggered does not remove the trigger. Transform:
which stimuli can be modified (removed, rescheduled, avoided without cost),
and which must be worked with because avoidance would narrow the client's
life? / Spheres of Influence (7 min). Introduce the three circles — control,
influence, out of reach — and sort the top 5 triggers. This is preview work;
the full Spheres exercise comes in Session 8. / Homework (3 min). Modify one
removable stimulus this week. Track what happens to the activation arc. Bring
the data to Session 6."

Key teaching moves (verbatim):

> "A trigger is not a character flaw. It's a cue your nervous system has
> learned to respond to. The goal is not to stop being triggered — that's
> impossible. The goal is to know your triggers so they don't run you."
> "Some triggers are worth removing. Some have to be worked with, because
> removing them would shrink your life. We'll sort which is which."
> "Your IAL — your baseline before any trigger shows up — is a stimulus too.
> Sleep, nutrition, unresolved conflict, rushing — all of those raise the
> number before the day's first trigger even arrives."

Fidelity criteria: written Stimulus Map of 5 triggers; each sorted
control/influence/out-of-reach; one modification experiment named; "Clinician
did not overreach into Activation or Response work. ... Session 5 stays at the
Stimulus stage." Pitfalls: solving before mapping; underestimating internal
stimuli; over-pathologizing the trigger ("Triggers are information, not
defects.").

### Clinical considerations (Part 4, summary)

- Pharmacotherapy: naltrexone/acamprosate "medication is a tailwind; it does
  not do the hook work for you"; buprenorphine/methadone — SPARO work begins
  after physiological stabilization; GLP-1 RAs reduce craving and slow early
  reactivity "but does not remove the underlying hooks. The hooks that remain
  after craving subsides are typically the most important to treat";
  psychiatric meds run on their own track — don't wait for stability.
- Trauma-informed, not trauma-focused. When trauma-origin hooks are active,
  "begin with Activation regulation (labeling, Spheres of Influence,
  stress-as-enhancing reappraisal) before Perception work," coordinate
  trauma-focused care (EMDR, PE, somatic) in parallel.
- Substance-class hook profiles: alcohol (emotional regulation, social
  lubrication, identity, dormant trauma; case: Terry, Ch. 9); opioids
  (physical pain, trauma, existential/meaning); stimulants
  (achievement/performance anxiety, productivity identity, sexual behavior
  hooks, sleep/energy); cannabis (anxiety management, social/performance
  anxiety — Linda, Ch. 2; "often misidentified by clients as 'not a real
  addiction'"); nicotine (stress regulation, ritual/transition cues,
  identity); polysubstance ("each substance serving a different hook. Map each
  substance's function separately before integration").
- Referral/pause/termination rules for suicide/homicide risk, psychosis/mania,
  pregnancy with active use, mid-treatment goal change (honor, document,
  re-baseline), repeated no-shows.

### Fidelity, supervision, measurement (Parts 5-6, summary)

- UMFS (Unhooked Method Fidelity Scale, v0.1 preview): dimensions rated 1-5 on
  10% audio sample + clinician self-report every session — framework use;
  stance (EAT: "curiosity without judgment ... No moralizing, no
  pathologizing, no unsolicited imposition of abstinence"); stage
  appropriateness; "Start at the Outcome" discipline; protocol adherence;
  clinician reactivity.
- Supervision: certification phase — weekly 60-min group + monthly 30-min
  individual; post-certification — monthly group for year one. Case
  presentation structure: SPARO map of client, current EAT stage, UMFS
  self-rating, session challenges, clinician's own reactivity.
- Outcome battery at baseline / post-Session 12 / 30-60-90-day follow-up:
  TLFB, craving scale, Hooks Inventory, Activation Thermometer self-report
  (IAL, flooding frequency, "% of episodes in which flooding was avoided via
  labeling/reappraisal"), PHQ-9, GAD-7, PCL-5, WHOQOL-BREF, Success Index,
  satisfaction battery.

## 2. Research_Collaborator_Brief.docx (v1.1)

Purpose: outreach brief seeking an academic co-investigator for a pilot
outcomes study of the Unhooked Method. Key framework statements (verbatim):

> "The Hooks model specifies what drives persistent behavior (the hook as
> underlying emotional, psychological, or situational driver). The SPARO / EAT
> system specifies how change is produced: SPARO (Stimulus → Perception →
> Activation → Response → Outcome) maps the behavioral chain, and EAT
> (Explore → Accept → Transform) is the transformation cycle applied at each
> stage of the chain."

> "Hooks are not the behavior — they are what the behavior is serving. The
> clinical consequence: treatment that targets the behavior without addressing
> the hook produces substitution (the hook migrates to a new behavior) or
> return (the original behavior recurs)."

> "The hook lives at the Perception–Activation junction."

> "EAT is iterative — a new Transform insight often surfaces a new layer of
> shame requiring another Accept pass."

(Note: that last sentence — Transform insights surfacing new Accept work — is
stated here more explicitly than in the manual.)

Study design summary:

- Stage 1: "a pragmatic, pre-registered, single-arm pilot with a pre/post
  repeated-measures design and 90-day follow-up," N≈60 treatment-seeking
  outpatient adults (21+) from IGNTD's pipeline. Primary feasibility outcome:
  retention through 12 sessions. Primary clinical outcomes: TLFB past-30-day
  use + modality-appropriate craving (PACS/QSU/DDQ). Secondary: PHQ-9, GAD-7,
  PCL-5, WHOQOL-BREF, Success Index, Hooks Inventory. Fidelity: clinician
  self-report every session + independent rating of 10% of recorded sessions.
- Stage 2 (contingent): RCT vs. treatment-as-usual or matched active control.
- Mechanistic research question (verbatim): "Is there preliminary signal for
  specific change on the mechanistic targets of the model — hook awareness,
  activation regulation (via the Activation Thermometer), perception shifts
  (belief buckets, cognitive biases), and replacement behavior adoption —
  above and beyond generalized SUD outcomes?"
- Timeline: months 1-3 IRB/manual lock/training/registration (OSF +
  ClinicalTrials.gov); 4-9 enrollment and delivery; 10-12 follow-up and
  analyses; 13-15 manuscript (targets: Addiction, Drug and Alcohol Dependence,
  Psychology of Addictive Behaviors) and conference (CPDD, APA Div 50,
  NIDA/NIAAA-adjacent).
- IP: "Underlying IP of the Hooks, SPARO, and EAT frameworks remains with
  Jaffe/IGNTD." First authorship open to the collaborator or trainee.
- Scale claim: the method "has delivered a structured version of this
  treatment to approximately 2,000 clients over the past decade."

"The Living Strategic Anchor" for the Unhooked Method System project.

North Star (verbatim): "Make the Unhooked Method — Hooks, SPARO, EAT — a
recognized operational system for substance use disorder treatment: cited in
peer-reviewed literature, usable by clinicians who have never met Adi,
measurable against validated outcomes, and durable beyond Adi's career."

Core thesis: frameworks that became evidence-based practice (MI, DBT, ACT,
CBT, IFS, TF-CBT) ran an academic/evidence track and a cultural/adoption track
in parallel; Adi is strong on cultural, "underbuilt on academic (no
peer-reviewed outcome paper on the method itself, no manualized protocol, no
validated measure). This project closes the academic gap."

Eight ingredients of sticky frameworks audit (status as of April 2026):
problem statement strong; named mechanism strong ("Hooks, SPARO, EAT are
already sticky names"); manual v0.2 exists (needs Sessions 2-4, 6-12);
validated instrument not started; published research none; training system
partial; community of practice none ("hardest and most important");
institutional endorsement downstream.

Three prioritized moves, in order:
1. "Publish one peer-reviewed pilot paper within 18 months" — pragmatic
   single-arm pilot, N≈60, co-authored with an academic collaborator.
2. "Lock the practitioner manual and validate the Hooks Inventory (in
   parallel)" — "The manual is the spine." Hooks Inventory items "come from
   existing IP — the 5 Belief Buckets, 9 cognitive biases, Activation
   Thermometer (IAL, safe zone, flooding threshold), and P/I Ratio across
   Psychology/Biology/Environment/Spirituality."
3. "Launch a tiered training pathway with supervision (after manual is
   locked)" — provisional tiers: "Unhooked-Informed Coach, Unhooked-Certified
   Clinician, Unhooked Method Practitioner." Stays inside IGNTD.

Temptation list (do NOT): no separate institute/non-profit; no training before
manual lock; no SAMHSA registry push now ("post-RCT move — probably 2029 at
earliest"); no CE/professional-society chase pre-publication; "Do not start
Book 4. The manual is Book 4, written for a different reader."

18-month timeline: collaborator selected + manual v1.0 + Hooks Inventory
psychometrics Q4 2026; IRB/recruitment Q1 2027; enrollment complete Q3 2027;
manuscript submitted Q1 2028; training pathway v1.0 Q1 2028.

Six pending decisions (collaborator targets, writer budget $40-80K, IGNTD
client-pool acceptability, registration entity, naming/trademarks, whether the
Success Index becomes a formal instrument). Risk register: cost/access equity
(Medicaid parity), Adi as single point of failure, adverse publication timing,
"mission drift into self-help."
