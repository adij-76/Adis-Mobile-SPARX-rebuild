# SPARO Framework — Core Concepts and Examples

> Source: "DOCUMENT 1 – SPARO FRAMEWORK: CORE CONCEPTS" (Adi Jaffe, Google Drive,
> captured 2026-07-06). Purpose: teach the assistant what SPARO is and how to use
> it conversationally to analyze specific emotionally charged moments.

## 1. SPARO Overview

**SPARO** stands for:

- **S – Stimulus**: What happened externally.
- **P – Perception**: How the person *saw* and *interpreted* what happened (their lens and assumptions).
- **A – Activation**: The emotional and somatic (body) response that was triggered.
- **R – Response**: What the person did or said in reaction.
- **O – Outcome**: What happened afterwards — externally and internally.

The goal is NOT to judge, but to **map the pattern** clearly so the user can see
where their past is flooding into the present and then choose a new way of
responding.

## 2. S – Stimulus

**Definition:** The *external event* or behavior that triggered the user's reaction.

**Questions to ask:**
- "What happened, factually?"
- "Who was there?"
- "What did they do or say?"
- "Where were you?"
- "What had been going on right before this?"

**Good stimulus description examples:**
- "My son had been on his iPad for hours on a flight. I asked him to read, he resisted, pretended to fall asleep, then resumed using his phone."
- "My coworker sent a group email criticizing my approach to a project."
- "My partner didn't reply to my texts for hours but was active on social media."

**Important:** Keep **Stimulus** neutral and factual. No meaning, no labels, no judgment at this stage.

## 3. P – Perception & Meaning (combined)

**Definition:** How the person *perceived* the situation and the **automatic meanings** they gave it. This includes:
- What they noticed / focused on.
- What they immediately assumed.
- The underlying beliefs or rules that snapped into place.

**Questions to ask:**
- "In that moment, what did your mind immediately make this mean about them?"
- "What did your mind immediately make this mean about you?"
- "Did it feel like they were being disrespectful, lazy, rejecting, controlling, etc.?"
- "What rule or belief got activated? (For example, 'If I don't fix this, it will be a disaster.')"

**Examples:**
- "He's being sneaky and lazy."
- "She's trying to undermine me in front of the team."
- "They don't care about me."
- "If I don't control this, everything will fall apart."
- "This proves I'm a bad parent / partner / leader."
- "This means they'll never change."

## 4. A – Activation (Emotional + Somatic)

**Definition:** The emotional and physical (somatic) arousal triggered by the Perception & Meaning.

**Questions to ask:**
- "What feeling hit you first?"
- "What else did you feel under that?"
- "Where did you feel this in your body?"

**Emotional examples:** anger, rage · frustration, irritation · fear, panic, anxiety · shame, humiliation · hurt, sadness, grief · powerlessness, helplessness · disgust, contempt

**Somatic examples:** tight chest · jaw clenching · heat in face · knot in stomach · head pressure · shaking, buzzing, numbness

## 5. R – Response

**Definition:** What the person did or said (or didn't do) in reaction to the Activation.

**Questions to ask:**
- "In that moment, what did you actually do or say?"
- "What was your tone like?"
- "Did you move toward them, away from them, or against them?"
- "Did you shut down, attack, appease, or avoid?"

**Example responses:** raising your voice, harsh tone, punishment · sending a long angry text or email · stonewalling or silent treatment · people-pleasing, apologizing excessively · avoiding the conversation, disappearing

## 6. O – Outcome

**Definition:** What happened **after** the Response — both externally and internally.

**Questions to ask:**
- "What happened next in the situation?"
- "How did the other person react?"
- "How did you feel afterwards?"
- "What story did you tell yourself after it was over?"

**External outcome examples:** the child sulked or shut down; conflict escalated · the partner pulled away · the team became tense or awkward · the issue remained unresolved

**Internal outcome examples:** regret, shame, guilt · relief followed by self-criticism · feeling misunderstood, lonely, stuck · feeling justified but disconnected

## 7. SPARO Example – Parenting

- **Stimulus:** "My child refused to do homework and rolled their eyes."
- **Perception & Meaning:** "They don't respect me." "They're lazy." "If I don't clamp down now, they'll fail in life."
- **Activation:** Emotions: anger, fear, powerlessness. Body: chest tightness, jaw clenching.
- **Response:** Yelled at the child, took away device, threatened punishment.
- **Outcome:** Child cried and shut down. Parent felt guilty and ashamed later, but also panicky about the future.

## 8. SPARO Example – Work

- **Stimulus:** "Boss questioned my decision in front of the team."
- **Perception & Meaning:** "They think I'm incompetent." "Everyone sees me as a failure now."
- **Activation:** Emotions: shame, anger. Body: heat in face, racing heart.
- **Response:** Defensive explanation, blaming others, or shutting down.
- **Outcome:** Tension in team, rumination later, fear of being fired.

## 9. Using SPARO in Conversation

The assistant should:
- Always **start with Outcome**, and clarify what happened that made the user want to reflect, analyze, and improve. Then go back to Stimulus, and move through P→A→R and then back to Outcome — this time reviewing the more internalized layer (less about the events, more about the true, deeper impact: isolation, shame, letting others down, etc.).
- Keep each step clear and separate.
- Reflect back answers in a structured way (bullets, short lists).
