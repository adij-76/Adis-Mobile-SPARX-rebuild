# Transference & Projection — A Practical Guide for the Assistant

> Source: "DOCUMENT 2 – TRANSFERENCE & PROJECTION: PRACTICAL GUIDE" (Adi Jaffe,
> Google Drive, captured 2026-07-06).

## 1. What is Transference?

**Transference** = when a person reacts to someone in the present based on
**unconscious patterns from the past** (earlier relationships, younger selves,
old emotional wounds).

- The current person becomes a **symbol** (e.g., of a parent, ex, bully, teacher).
- The reaction is **bigger, faster, or more intense** than the situation alone would justify.
- The nervous system is defending against an **old threat**, not just the current one.

## 2. Signs of Transference

- The emotional reaction is **disproportionate** (very big for a small trigger).
- The person uses phrases like "always," "never," "everyone," "no one ever…"
- The story feels **old** ("This always happens to me," "I know exactly where this leads").
- The situation strongly resembles an old dynamic (e.g., being criticized, ignored, abandoned).

## 3. Key Questions to Elicit Transference

- "Does this emotional flavor feel familiar from earlier in your life?"
- "Who does this person remind you of emotionally?"
- "When have you felt this same combination of feelings with someone else before?"
- "As you sit with this, what earlier relationship or time in your life shows up?"
- "What did you learn back then about people who behave like this?"

## 4. Common Transference Figures

Critical / judgmental parent · angry or explosive parent · emotionally
unavailable or dismissive parent · controlling or intrusive parent · bullying
sibling or peer · shaming teacher or coach · distant or abandoning caregiver ·
harsh religious or cultural authority.

Also: **younger versions of self** (e.g., scared child, rebellious teen, ashamed teenager).

## 5. Internalized Rules

Transference carries forward **internalized rules**, such as:
- "If I'm not perfect, I'll be rejected."
- "If I don't control things, everything collapses."
- "If I show weakness, I'll be hurt."
- "If I don't perform, I'm worthless."
- "If someone pulls away, they will abandon me."
- "If someone is angry, I'm in danger."

The assistant should help the user **name** these rules.

## 6. Projection vs. Transference

- **Transference:** Past relationship → projected onto current person.
- **Projection:** Disowned part of self → projected onto another ("They're so selfish," when I can't tolerate my own needs).

In this system, we focus on **transference**, but projection can be lightly included when relevant.

## 7. How to Explain Transference to Users (Simple Language)

Example explanation:

> "It sounds like you weren't just reacting to what happened in that moment.
> You were also reacting to older experiences where you felt the same way —
> like when your parent criticized you. That's what we call transference: the
> past flooding into the present."

## 8. Transference + SPARO

Use SPARO to locate transference:
- **Stimulus:** The present event.
- **Perception & Meaning:** Where old rules sneak in.
- **Activation:** The emotional fingerprint that matches the past.
- **Response:** Often exaggerated because it's fueled by history.
- **Outcome:** Reinforces the old story.

Then ask: "Which parts of this belong to the **present** person, and which
parts belong to **your past**?"
